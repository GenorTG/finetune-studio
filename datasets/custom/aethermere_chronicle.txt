# THE AETHERMERE CHRONICLE
## Annals of the Disc from the First Landing to the Present Age

---

## THE FOUNDRY EPOCH (Years 1–412)

### The First Landing (Year 1)

The first humans arrived on Aethermere in three ships — the *Ossuary Bell*, the *Kindly Lantern*, and the *Salt Psalm* — carrying 4,112 settlers across the Void Sea from a homeland no surviving record names. They landed at a natural harbor on the disc's eastern rim and founded the city of Vantage, whose original stone seawall still stands, forty feet tall and twelve feet thick at the base.

The first winter killed 618 settlers. The survivors attributed their salvation to the Luminous Ring, whose light was said to have burned away a plague of silver-moths that had infested the grain stores. The Order of the Ring was founded that same spring by the settler Heza Marun, and its first sanctum was a converted grain-silo that still exists in Vantage's Lower Ward as the museum called the Silo of Gratitude.

### The Gorath Wars (Years 89–103)

In Year 89, the Gorath of the Underside tunneled through the disc's crust in eleven places simultaneously, opening shafts between nine and thirty feet wide. The largest breach, near the town of Brindle, swallowed an entire cattle market along with 204 people and 380 head of livestock. The war that followed lasted fourteen years and is remembered for three engagements:

1. **The Battle of Tallow Field (Year 91)** — 3,000 human militia under Commander Veru Ash held a Gorath probing force of unknown numbers for six hours until the Gorath withdrew at dawn. Ash's forces lost 412 dead; the field, rendered for a century afterward, still turns up bone-white chitin fragments after plowing.
2. **The Sinking of Cormorant's Rest (Year 97)** — the Gorath undercut the town's foundations; it slid into the Void Sea with 1,730 inhabitants. Its bell tower, detached and afloat, was found eight months later drifting 60 miles off the rim. The bell was recovered and now hangs in Vantage's Cathedral of the Ring; it weighs two tons and is rung only on Remembrance Day, the 17th of Frostmonth.
3. **The Sealings (Years 101–103)** — Aetherweavers of the School of Gravitation collapsed the eleven Gorath shafts by density-anchoring the crust, a working so large that the forty weavers who cast it each sacrificed their memories of their own children. The Sealers' Monument in Brindle lists all forty names; it is nine feet tall and made of fused Gorath chitin.

The Gorath Wars ended with no treaty — the Gorath simply stopped breaching. Scholars still argue whether the Sealings taught them the cost or whether some deeper migration drew them off. Recorded Gorath fatalities across the entire war total 3,911; human dead were 6,204.

### The Weaving Accords (Year 240)

By Year 240, unlicensed Aetherweaving had hollowed an estimated one in twelve nobles into Husks. The Weaving Accords, signed in Vantage on the 3rd of Seedmonth, established:

- **The Ledger of Memory** — every licensed weaver must record, before witnesses, each memory they intend to spend, so that the person they become can be tracked. The Ledger's original volumes, 412 of them, are kept in the Vault of Quiet beneath Vantage.
- **The Five-School License** — a weaver may be licensed in at most two Schools. Practicing a third School is the crime of Overreach, punishable by Binding (the surgical sealing of one's Aether-channels) — not by death, because the Accords' framers considered a weaver who cannot remember why they wanted power to be punishment enough.
- **The Tithe of Remembrance** — every settlement above 500 souls must maintain a Remembrancer, a paid official whose duty is to memorize the biographies of local weavers and speak them aloud each year, so the community remembers who the weaver was, not just what they are. Remembrancers are paid 40 crowns a year from the municipal purse.

The Accords have been amended four times: in 388 (adding the Husk-warden statutes), in 641 (the Umbral Annex, after the Night of Doors), in 1144 (the Reckoning Clauses), and in 1602 (the present Concord of Two Crowns).

## THE AGE OF SAIL (Years 412–890)

### The Driftmass Trade (Years 450–700)

When salvagers learned that Driftmasses could be temporarily anchored with gravitation-workings, a rush began. A stabilized Driftmass yields **driftglass** — solidified possibility, worth roughly 300 crowns per pound in Vantage — and anchoring rights were auctioned by the Crown of Vantage from Year 455 onward.

The trade's golden century produced the Salvage Codes of 512, still the basis of maritime law:

- Salvage crews must be licensed by the Admiralty Court in Vantage; a license costs 80 crowns and demands a bond of 400.
- The first ship to drop anchor within sight of an unclaimed Driftmass holds priority for one tide (roughly six hours).
- Crews share prize money by fixed shares: the captain takes 12 parts, the mate 6, the gravitation-weaver 8, each sailor 2, the ship's boy 1. A typical successful voyage in Year 600 netted each sailor about 55 crowns — three years' shore wages.
- Any crew that loses a member to a vanishing Driftmass must pay the family 200 crowns or forfeit its license.

The worst year of the trade was 587, called the Year of Widows, when 17 ships and 690 sailors were lost to phase-outs. The trade collapsed in Year 700 when the Isle of Quietus, newly anchored, was found to *resist* stabilization — eleven anchoring crews (141 people) vanished over three seasons, and the Admiralty Court declared Quietus unanchorable in perpetuity.

### The Night of Doors (Year 641)

On the night of the 21st of Veilmonth, 641, every door in the city of Cormorant — population 9,000 — opened at once, and something crossed the thresholds in the dark. By morning, 309 residents were missing and 44 were found alive but unable to remember their own names; the 44 are the founding case-study of Umbral research. The city's gates were sealed by order of Archon Pell Ravas, and the Cormorant Quarantine has held for a thousand years: the city stands, inhabited by descendants of the 8,647 who chose to stay inside, ruled by the Door-Wardens, and visited by supply-barge on the first of each month. No one born inside the Quarantine has ever been permitted out; the fine for smuggling a person out is 10,000 crowns and Binding.

The Night of Doors produced the Umbral Annex to the Weaving Accords (Year 641), which requires every city of over 2,000 people to maintain salt-lined threshold-grooves beneath every door, renewed monthly. The salt trade that followed made the mining town of Greyhallow rich: its pans produce 4,000 pounds of threshold-salt a year, sold at 2 crowns the pound.

## THE LONG REGENCY (Years 890–1144)

### The Hollowing of the Crown (Year 890)

Queen Sela III died in 890 without an heir, having spent her memories extravagantly in the last decade of her life — court records show 2,206 entries in her personal Ledger, culminating in her final working, which erased every memory of her own reign. The Regency Council of Nine was seated to rule until "a claimant of unstained memory presents the Ring's own token." No claimant ever has.

The Council of Nine — three weavers, three Remembrancers, three admirals — has ruled for 254 years at this writing. Its seat is the Council Spire in Vantage, 200 feet tall, and its votes are recorded on the Nine Pillars, one pillar per councilor, each vote carved fresh by a stonemason paid 3 crowns per session.

### The Reckoning (Years 1139–1144)

A weaver-cult called the Unburdened, preaching that memory itself was a cage, spent five years deliberately mass-Hollowing volunteers: 1,144 people made themselves Husks in staged ceremonies, and the Husks wandered the Sallow Marches in a pack that grew bolder each season. The Reckoning Clauses of 1144 were the response:

- Husk-making is now the crime of Feeding the Pack, punishable by Binding and the confiscation of all property to the victims' fund.
- The Marches Wardens were founded — 60 mounted wardens, paid 90 crowns a year, who patrol the Sallow Marches and cull Husk packs. A warden's pension, after 20 years, is 30 crowns a year for life.
- Every Ledger entry made in the previous ten years was audited; 331 weavers were Bound for unrecorded spendings.

The pack itself was broken at the Battle of the Sallow Ford in 1142, where 900 militia and 40 wardens ended the wandering of the 1,144. It took one day. The wardens' losses were 9; the militia's, 210. The Unburdened's founder, a weaver named Ilse Vantorre, was never caught; her last verified sighting was in 1147 at the rim-town of Pennhallow, and the reward for her capture — 5,000 crowns, posted in 1144 — has never been paid.

## THE PRESENT CONCORD (Years 1602–now)

The Concord of Two Crowns (Year 1602) joined the crowns of Vantage and Brindle under Regent-Queen Maren the Patient, first monarch in 712 years to hold executive power. Maren's Court sits in Brindle's Hall of Seals, and the crown's annual budget — 2.1 million crowns — is published openly each Seedmonth, a practice Maren began and which historians call the Quiet Revolution in governance.

Key figures of the present year (1666 by the Vantine reckoning):

- **Regent-Queen Maren the Patient**, crowned 1602 at age 29; she is now 93 and has ruled 64 years. Her personal Ledger has 12 entries — she is the least-weaving monarch in memory.
- **First Councilor Edric Solm**, chair of the Council of Nine since 1649, formerly Admiralty Court judge.
- **Grand Remembrancer Tuva Ash**, titular descendant of Commander Veru Ash, keeper of the Vault of Quiet; her office employs 118 Remembrancers across the realm.
- **Warden-General Osric Bell**, commander of the Marches Wardens since 1651; the Wardens number 72 as of this year.
- **Harbor-Admiral Sef Quill**, holder of the Admiralty Seals; the driftglass trade has revived under his court, 30 licensed crews as of this year, each bond 600 crowns.

The realm's population is approximately 310,000. Vantage holds 41,000; Brindle 22,000; Greyhallow 8,400; the remainder live in the rim-towns and farmsteads. The disc's total settled coastline is 600 miles; beyond it lies the Void Sea and, eventually, the Luminous Ring, which no expedition has reached — the furthest voyage, the *Salt Psalm*'s second crossing in 1509, sailed 900 miles off the rim and returned with all hands mad from the light, 41 souls ruined in a single season.
