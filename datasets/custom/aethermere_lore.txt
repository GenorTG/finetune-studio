# THE CODEX OF AETHERMERE
## Official Lore Compendium of the Eternal Realms

---

## I. COSMOLOGY

Aethermere is a flat disc-world suspended in the Void Sea, encircled by the Luminous Ring — a band of crystallized starlight that prevents the oceans from pouring into the Abyss. The disc rotates slowly around its central axis, creating day and night as the sun (Sol Aeternus) passes beneath the disc and rises again from the east. The underside of the disc is known as the Underside, a hostile realm of inverted gravity and predatory crystal forests, inhabited by the Gorath — eyeless, silicon-based lifeforms that navigate through echolocation and vibration.

The Void Sea is not empty. It contains islands of compressed possibility known as Driftmasses, which phase in and out of existence. Sailors who venture too far from Aethermere's edge risk sailing into a Driftmass that vanishes, leaving them adrift in the Void. The largest permanent Driftmass is the Isle of Quietus, a necropolis-city where the dead are said to await reincarnation. No living person has ever returned from Quietus with verifiable proof of what lies there.

### The Three Planes

Aethermere exists at the intersection of three planes: the Material (where physical beings dwell), the Aether (a parallel dimension of pure thought and magical energy), and the Umbral (the shadow-plane where fears and forgotten things reside). Magic is the practice of drawing energy from the Aether into the Material plane, but every such draw creates a corresponding disturbance in the Umbral — this is the fundamental law known as the Equilibrium Debt.

---

## II. THE MAGIC SYSTEM: AETHERWEAVING

Aetherweaving is not innate. Anyone can learn it, but the cost is memory. Every spell cast consumes a specific, personal memory from the caster's mind. The more powerful the spell, the more significant the memory consumed. A simple light-spell might erase the memory of what you ate for breakfast. A resurrection spell consumes the memory of your first love's face. This is why master Aetherweavers are often hollow, distant people — they have traded their pasts for power.

There are five Schools of Aetherweaving:

1. **Luminance** — light, heat, and energy manipulation. Memory cost: sensory memories (tastes, textures, colors).
2. **Gravitation** — weight, density, and spatial distortion. Memory cost: spatial memories (the layout of your childhood home, the route to a friend's house).
3. **Vivomancy** — healing, growth, and biological manipulation. Memory cost: emotional memories (the feeling of being loved, the grief of a loss).
4. **Temporality** — time dilation, precognition, and temporal anchoring. Memory cost: sequential memories (the order of events in your life, names of acquaintances).
5. **Onirics** — dreamwalking, illusion, and Umbral navigation. Memory cost: identity memories (your name, your sense of self, your values).

Aetherweavers who practice multiple schools risk Memory Collapse — a condition where so many memories are consumed that the caster loses coherence and becomes a Husk. Husks are wandering, spell-casting bodies with no personality, no past, and no restraint. They are among the most dangerous creatures in Aethermere.

## III. THE GREAT HOUSES AND POLITICAL STRUCTURE

### House Valdris (The Iron Merchants)
Seat: Valdris Hold, northern mountain range
Sigil: A silver anvil on black
House Valdris controls the mining and trade of Aetherium — the crystallized form of Aetheric energy used to power everything from streetlamps to warships. They are the wealthiest house but have no magical tradition of their own, instead hiring Aetherweavers as mercenaries. Their current matriarch, **Seraphina Valdris**, is 147 years old and has allegedly extended her life through a pact with the Gorath of the Underside. She has not been seen in public for thirty years.

### House Theron (The Stormcallers)
Seat: The Floating Citadel of Kael, which hovers above the central ocean
Sigil: A white lightning bolt on deep blue
House Theron breeds Aetherweavers. Through centuries of selective marriage and genetic tracking, they produce more talented weavers than any other house. Their bloodline is the only one known to naturally produce dual-school weavers. The current head, **Lord Cassian Theron**, is a master of both Gravitation and Vivomancy, making him one of the most powerful living weavers. He is 6'8", missing his left arm (lost in the Sundering War), and speaks in a whisper that can be heard across a crowded room.

### House Maren (The Dreamkeepers)
Seat: The Labyrinth, a city built inside a single enormous tree in the eastern forests
Sigil: An open eye within a spiral
House Maren specializes in Onirics and maintains the Dream Network — a shared dreamspace where messages can be sent across vast distances instantaneously. They are the information brokers of Aethermere, and their intelligence network is unmatched. The current head is **Sylara Maren**, a blind woman who sees only through the eyes of others in the Dream Network. She is accompanied everywhere by her bonded companion, a Voidcat named Whisper who can phase through solid matter.

### House Kael (The Voidwalkers)
Seat: The Anchor, a fortress built on the edge of the disc overlooking the Void Sea
Sigil: A broken chain on grey
House Kael patrols the edge of the world, defending against incursions from Driftmasses and Void Sea creatures. They are sailors, explorers, and the closest thing Aethermere has to a navy. Their ships are powered by bound Aetheric crystals and can temporarily sail the Void Sea if the navigator is skilled enough. The current head is **Captain Orion Kael**, a man who has sailed to seven different Driftmasses and returned — more than any other living navigator. He is missing his shadow, which was stolen by an Umbral entity during his third voyage.

### House Voss (The Memory Keepers)
Seat: The Archive, an underground library beneath the capital city of Concordia
Sigil: A golden key on white
House Voss records and preserves memories — both their own and those volunteered (or stolen) from others. They are the historians and archivists of Aethermere, and their library contains memories dating back over two thousand years, stored in Aetheric crystals. The current head is **Archivist Elara Voss**, who carries the memories of seventeen previous Archivists within her, giving her a fractured but vast consciousness. She sometimes refers to herself in the plural.

## IV. GEOGRAPHY

### Concordia
The capital city, located at the exact center of the disc. It is governed by the Council of Five (one representative from each Great House) and is the only place in Aethermere where all five schools of Aetherweaving may be legally practiced. Concordia is built in concentric rings around the Spire of Accord — a 900-foot tower of pure Aetherium that serves as the seat of government and the primary source of the city's power. The outer ring, known as the Thorns, is the poorest district, where failed Aetherweavers and Husks are exiled.

### The Sunless Reaches
The northernmost region, where Sol Aeternus's light barely reaches. It is a frozen wasteland populated by ice-dwelling creatures and the remnants of House Valdris's failed mining colonies. The Reaches are said to contain a dormant Temporality anomaly — a place where time stopped completely during the Sundering War and has not resumed.

### The Verdance
The eastern forest, home to the Labyrinth and House Maren. The trees here are sentient (though not sapient) and communicate through root networks that extend for thousands of miles. The forest is considered sacred, and logging is punishable by exile to the Thorns.

### The Rust
A massive desert in the south, covered in the ruins of a pre-Aethermere civilization that predates recorded history. The ruins contain artifacts that do not conform to any known magical theory — devices that appear to function without Aetheric energy, structures built from materials not found anywhere else in the world. House Voss has been excavating the Rust for centuries but has released almost no findings to the public.

### The Shattered Coast
The western shore, where the disc meets the Void Sea. It is perpetually stormy, as the boundary between the Material plane and the Void creates unpredictable weather patterns. House Kael maintains a chain of lighthouses along the coast, each powered by a bound Aetherweaver who sacrifices their memories to keep the light burning for their term of service (typically ten years).

## V. HISTORY

### The Age of Silence (Before Year 0)
Before the invention of Aetherweaving, Aethermere was a dark, cold world. Humans survived in small tribes, huddled around natural Aetheric vents for warmth. The Aether existed but could not be accessed. This era lasted an unknown length of time.

### The First Weaving (Year 0)
A woman named **Lyra the Forgotten** discovered how to draw Aetheric energy into the Material plane by accident — she was attempting to light a fire and instead tore open a hole in reality. The process consumed her memory of her own name, but she had invented Aetherweaving. She spent the rest of her life (approximately forty years) teaching others the technique, though she never remembered any of their names.

### The Crystal Bloom (Year 247)
Aetherium was discovered in the northern mountains, triggering a gold rush. House Valdris was founded to manage the mining operations. The Crystal Bloom led to rapid technological advancement but also to the first recorded cases of Memory Collapse.

### The War of Echoes (Year 512-538)
The first major conflict between Houses, fought over control of Aetherium deposits. House Theron and House Kael allied against House Valdris and House Voss. The war ended in a stalemate after eighteen years, but it established the balance of power that persists to this day. Approximately 200,000 people died, and the first Husks were created during this war as desperate Aetherweavers burned through their memories to gain advantage.

### The Night of Black Fire (Year 781)
A catastrophic Temporality experiment by an unnamed Voss Archivist caused a localized time-loop in the capital city of Concordia. For seven days, the entire city relived the same twenty-four hours. When the loop finally broke, 10,000 people had vanished — pulled into the Umbral plane by the Equilibrium Debt. Their bodies remained, alive but empty, and these became the first Husks created by temporal magic rather than memory overuse.

### The Sundering War (Year 1024-1032)
The most devastating conflict in Aethermere's history. House Theron attempted to seize control of the Spire of Accord, which would have given them monopoly over Concordia's power supply. The other four Houses united against them. The war ended when Lord Cassian Theron's great-great-grandfather, **Theron the Unbound**, cast a spell of such magnitude that it consumed every memory he had ever formed — including the memory of how to be human. He became the most powerful Husk ever recorded, a being of pure magical energy that nearly destroyed Concordia before being sealed beneath the Spire by a coalition of the most powerful weavers from all five Houses.

### The Reconstruction (Year 1032-Present)
The current era. A fragile peace has held for over two centuries, but tensions remain. House Theron, though defeated, retained their Floating Citadel and their breeding programs. House Valdris's wealth grows every year. House Maren's Dream Network has made them indispensable but also feared. House Kael's patrols grow more desperate as Driftmass incursions increase in frequency. And House Voss continues to dig in the Rust, looking for something they will not name.

## VI. RELIGION AND PHILOSOPHY

### The Church of the Eternal Return
The dominant religion of Aethermere, based on the belief that all souls are recycled through the Isle of Quietus and reborn into new bodies. The Church teaches that the memories consumed by Aetherweaving are not destroyed but transferred to the Isle of Quietus, where they are stored until the soul is reborn. This doctrine gives comfort to Aetherweavers who have lost their pasts — they believe they will recover what was lost in their next life. The Church is headquartered in Concordia and is led by the **Voice of Quietus**, a position that has been held by the same person — **Elias Thorne** — for over 300 years. Whether he has literally lived that long or whether the title is passed down is a matter of theological debate.

### The Unwritten
A heretical philosophy that argues the Equilibrium Debt can be cheated — that there is a way to cast magic without losing memories. The Unwritten believe that the Isle of Quietus is not a waystation for reincarnation but a prison, and that the memories consumed by Aetherweaving are consumed by an entity that lives there. They seek to find this entity and destroy it, believing that doing so will free all consumed memories and end the need for the memory cost. The Church considers them blasphemous and has outlawed their teachings, but their underground following grows, particularly among failed Aetherweavers who have lost too much.

### The Gorath Faith
Practiced by the eyeless beings of the Underside, this is a religion of vibration and resonance. The Gorath believe that the universe is a single, sustained note, and that all matter is simply the interference pattern of this note. They do not worship gods — they worship the note itself. Their priests, known as Resonators, can vibrate their bodies at frequencies that allow them to move through solid matter, and the most powerful among them can shatter Aetherium crystals with a sustained hum.

## VII. TECHNOLOGY

Aethermere's technology is powered by Aetherium crystals, which store Aetheric energy. The crystals come in five grades:

1. **Dust** — powdered Aetherium, used in cheap lanterns and heaters. Lasts approximately one week before needing replacement.
2. **Shard** — small crystals, used in personal devices like Dream Network communicators and medical scanners. Lasts approximately one month.
3. **Core** — fist-sized crystals, used in vehicles (flying ships, ground transports) and building power systems. Lasts approximately one year.
4. **Heart** — human-head-sized crystals, used in military applications and industrial machinery. Lasts approximately five years.
5. **Tear of Sol** — extremely rare, person-sized crystals that are said to be fragments of Sol Aeternus itself. Only three are known to exist. One powers the Spire of Accord. One is held by House Theron and powers the Floating Citadel. The third was lost during the Sundering War and is rumored to be hidden somewhere in the Rust.

The process of refining raw Aetherium ore into usable crystals is a closely guarded secret of House Valdris. Refiners, known as **Crucible Workers**, are exposed to raw Aetheric energy daily and have an average life expectancy of only 45 years, most dying of Aetheric poisoning — a condition where the body begins to crystallize from the inside out.

### Notable Technologies

- **Dreamstones** — hand-sized crystals that allow access to the Dream Network. Owned by House Maren and leased to the public for a monthly fee.
- **Anchor Chains** — massive Aetheric chains that tether the Floating Citadel of Kael to the disc below, preventing it from drifting into the Void Sea.
- **Voidglass** — a transparent material forged from solidified Void Sea mist, used in House Kael's ships. It is the only material that can withstand the Void Sea's corrosive properties.
- **Memory Vials** — small crystal containers used by House Voss to store extracted memories. A single vial can hold approximately one year of memories.
- **Resonance Keys** — tuning-fork-like devices that can shatter Aetherium crystals at specific frequencies. The most destructive weapon in Aethermere, as they can disable any technology. Their manufacture is banned by the Council of Five.

## VIII. CREATURES AND RACES

### Humans
The dominant race, approximately 95% of the population. No inherent magical ability, but any human can learn Aetherweaving at the cost of memory.

### The Gorath
Silicon-based lifeforms from the Underside. Eyeless, gray-skinned, with elongated limbs and hollow bones. They navigate by echolocation and can sense vibrations through any material. They are not hostile by nature but are territorial and view surface-dwellers as thieves (since the Aetherium mined from the northern mountains is, geologically, an extension of the Underside). Their population is estimated at 50,000-100,000, though no accurate census has ever been taken.

### Voidcats
Semi-corporeal felines native to the Void Sea. They can phase through solid matter and are drawn to Aetheric energy sources. Some have been domesticated by Aetherweavers, but they remain unpredictable. They purr at a frequency that promotes healing, and their presence can slow the progression of Aetheric poisoning. House Maren has a breeding program for Voidcats, but they remain rare and expensive.

### Driftborn
Creatures that emerge from Driftmasses when they phase into existence. Their forms vary wildly — some are barely distinguishable from natural animals, while others are utterly alien. The most dangerous Driftborn are the **Echoes** — beings that appear to be copies of people who have been lost in Driftmasses, but are actually Void Sea entities wearing stolen identities. Echoes can mimic voices and mannerisms perfectly but cannot replicate memories — they know nothing of the person they are imitating.

### Luminari
Beings of pure light that inhabit the Luminous Ring. They are not hostile but are utterly incomprehensible — their motivations and communication methods are so alien that no human has ever successfully negotiated with them. They occasionally descend to the surface, and these visitations are considered omens by the Church of the Eternal Return.

### Husks
Former humans who have lost too many memories to function as individuals. They retain muscle memory and can perform complex tasks (including Aetherweaving) but have no personality, no preferences, and no restraint. They wander in packs, drawn to sources of Aetheric energy, and will attack if they feel threatened. The Thorns district of Concordia is home to an estimated 3,000 Husks, tolerated because they perform manual labor without complaint.

## IX. NOTABLE INDIVIDUALS

### Seraphina Valdris (Age 147)
Matriarch of House Valdris. A shrewd, ruthless businesswoman who has allegedly extended her life through a pact with the Gorath. She communicates through intermediaries and has not appeared in public in thirty years. Rumors suggest she has replaced so much of her body with Aetherium augments that she is more crystal than flesh.

### Lord Cassian Theron (Age 62)
Head of House Theron. A towering man missing his left arm, known for his whisper that carries across rooms. Master of Gravitation and Vivomancy. Widely considered the most powerful living Aetherweaver, though he has not cast a spell in public since his wife, **Elena Theron**, was killed in a Driftborn incursion twenty years ago. He has become increasingly isolationist, focusing House Theron's efforts on defending their Floating Citadel rather than expanding their influence.

### Sylara Maren (Age 34)
Head of House Maren. Born blind, she sees only through the Dream Network, using the eyes of others to perceive the world. She is bonded to Whisper, a Voidcat who can phase through solid matter. Sylara is a master of Onirics and is rumored to have entered the dreams of every member of the Council of Five at least once. She is the youngest head of a Great House in recorded history.

### Captain Orion Kael (Age 45)
Head of House Kael. A seasoned navigator who has sailed to seven Driftmasses and returned. Missing his shadow, stolen by an Umbral entity. He is grim, pragmatic, and deeply superstitious. He has forbidden any member of House Kael from sailing to the Isle of Quietus, and his standing order is to destroy any vessel that attempts the journey. Why he has this rule is unknown to anyone but him.

### Archivist Elara Voss (Age 78)
Head of House Voss. Carries the memories of seventeen previous Archivists within her, giving her a fractured consciousness. She sometimes speaks in the first-person plural and refers to events from centuries ago as if she witnessed them personally. She is the foremost expert on the Rust and its pre-Aethermere ruins, and she has been excavating there for over fifty years. Her ultimate goal — what she is looking for — she has never revealed.

### Elias Thorne / The Voice of Quietus (Age: disputed)
Religious leader of the Church of the Eternal Return. Has held his position for over 300 years, though whether it is literally the same man or a succession of title-holders is debated. He speaks rarely, and when he does, his words are recorded as scripture. His most famous pronouncement, made exactly 100 years ago: "The Debt comes due. The Ring fractures. The Forgotten returns." No one knows what this means, but it is interpreted differently by every faction.

### Lyra the Forgotten (Historical)
The inventor of Aetherweaving, who lived approximately 1,200 years ago. She lost her name in the First Weaving and spent the rest of her life (approximately forty years) teaching others the technique. She founded the first Aetherweaver academy in Concordia, which later became the basis for House Theron's breeding programs. She died without recovering her name, and the Church considers her a saint — the woman who gave humanity power at the cost of herself.

## X. THE DEBT CRISIS

For the past fifty years, the Equilibrium Debt — the fundamental law that every draw of Aetheric energy creates a disturbance in the Umbral — has been accelerating. More spells are being cast than ever before, and the Umbral disturbances are becoming more frequent and more severe. Driftmass incursions have increased by 300% in the last decade. Husks are being created faster than ever. The Luminous Ring has begun to flicker — something that has never happened in recorded history.

The Council of Five is divided on how to respond:

- **House Theron** proposes rationing Aetherweaving, limiting the amount of magic that can be cast per person per year.
- **House Valdris** proposes mining more Aetherium to "overpower" the Umbral disturbances with sheer energy.
- **House Maren** proposes entering the Dream Network en masse to seek answers from the collective unconscious.
- **House Kael** proposes sailing to the Isle of Quietus to negotiate with whatever entity lies there.
- **House Voss** proposes finding the third Tear of Sol in the Rust, believing it can stabilize the Ring.

None of these proposals have gained majority support. Meanwhile, the Debt continues to accumulate, and the world edges closer to a catastrophe that some believe will make the Sundering War look like a prelude.

## APPENDICES

### Appendix A: The Five Prohibitions (Laws of Aethermere)
1. No Aetherweaver may cast a spell that consumes another person's memories without consent.
2. No Husk may be created intentionally. Any weaver who creates a Husk through negligence is subject to exile in the Thorns.
3. No Temporality magic may be practiced within Concordia's city limits.
4. No Resonance Key may be manufactured, possessed, or sold.
5. No living person may set foot on the Isle of Quietus.

### Appendix B: Currency
Aethermere uses a currency called **Lumens**, which are small, standardized Aetherium coins. One lumen contains enough energy to power a light-spell for one hour. The exchange rate (maintained by House Valdris) is approximately:
- 1 Heart-grade crystal = 10,000 lumens
- 1 Core-grade crystal = 1,000 lumens
- 1 Shard-grade crystal = 100 lumens
- 1 Dust-grade crystal = 10 lumens

### Appendix C: Calendar
The Aethermere calendar has 360 days per year, divided into 12 months of 30 days each. The months are named after the five Great Houses in rotating order (Valdris, Theron, Maren, Kael, Voss, Valdris, Theron, Maren, Kael, Voss, Valdris, Theron). The current year is 1247 A.F. (After Forgotten).

### Appendix D: Languages
The common trade language is **Concordian**, a hybrid of the five Houses' original tongues. Each House retains its own ceremonial language:
- Valdris: **Old Iron** (guttural, suited for shouting over mining equipment)
- Theron: **Stormspeak** (tonal, with words that mimic wind patterns)
- Maren: **Dreamtongue** (largely non-verbal, using gestures and projected emotions)
- Kael: **Voidcant** (whisper-based, designed for communication during storms)
- Voss: **High Archive** (a written-only language with no spoken form)
